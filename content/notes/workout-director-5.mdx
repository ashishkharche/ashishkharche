When Workout Director counts a rep with the camera, it says the number out loud. Most reps get just the number. A few get a short phrase. This note covers the rule behind those phrases, why they are recorded clips, and the cases where the voice should stay quiet. It is about the count's own voice. The optional live coach, which talks with you and can change the workout, is a separate system and gets its own part later in the series; [part 1](/notes/workout-director-1/) gives the outline.

## The problem

During a set, people are looking at the floor, the bar or the wall, not the phone. The count has to reach them by sound. But a voice that says too much is worse than none: it talks over music, over a phone call, over the optional live coach. Each line had to be short, predictable and correct for the set's target.

## The rule

The wording comes from one pure function. For a set with a target:

```text
target − 1                         "N. Last one."
target                             "N. Done."
target − 3, sets of 6 or more      "N. Three more!"
half of an even target, 8 or more  "N. Halfway there!"
past the target                    just the number
the same number again              nothing
a one-rep set                      "1. Done."
```

For a set of 10 that gives: "1", "2", "3", "4", "5. Halfway there!", "6", "7. Three more!", "8", "9. Last one.", "10. Done."

For a set of 7, there is no halfway line, because 7 is odd. You hear "4. Three more!", "6. Last one." and "7. Done." The clip below is a set of 5, too short for "Three more!", so it goes 1, 2, 3, "4. Last one." and "5. Done."

## Clips, not live speech

Every line is a short audio clip bundled in the app as `coach_<key>.m4a`. There are clips for the numbers 1 to 50, the rep phrases, the partial-rep cues ("Go a bit lower.") and the framing hints ("Step back", "Turn sideways"). A longer line is built by joining clips: "9. Last one." plays `n9` and then `last_one`.

**The clips are AI-generated.** They were made once with a text-to-speech model and are shipped as files. Nothing is generated during a workout. The source comment gives the reason: the workout should never sound like the phone's built-in text-to-speech voice. A unit test walks through every line the app can say and fails if any of them has no clip.

## When to stay quiet

iOS tells an app what kind of spoken prompt is appropriate right now, through `AVAudioSession.promptStyle`:

- **Siri is listening** (`.none`): the count says nothing.
- **A phone call is active** (`.short`): the count becomes a short chime.
- **Otherwise**: the full line.

People can also switch counts to haptics or silent in Preferences, or say "mute the counts" to the live coach. Since 1.0.55 that choice sticks for later workouts.

## Bugs that shaped it

**A cue that repeated on every dropout.** If the camera lost sight of the person, the app used to say so every time tracking dropped. In a set with someone moving in and out of frame, that meant the same line again and again. Since 1.0.32 the line is spoken at most once per set. The framing pill on screen does the rest, and the count is left alone.

**Clips cutting the coach off.** Until a change on 22 October 2026, a count clip could cut into the live coach's call. Now the count clips duck under the coach instead. The live count still goes to the coach as text, so it knows the number without anyone saying it twice.

## Tradeoffs

- **A fixed voice can't adapt its wording.** The clips say exactly what was recorded. That is the point: the count has to be the same every time. The live coach covers anything that needs real sentences.
- **New phrases need new clips.** Adding a line means generating a clip, and the test makes sure that never gets forgotten.
- **Disclosure matters.** Because the voice is AI-generated, I say so wherever it is used, including in this note and in the clip.
