Workout Director is an iPhone and iPad training app that I built. It is published on the App Store by Shelgaon Ltd as "Workout Director - Gym & Home". You prop the phone up and press Start; the camera counts your reps and a voice coach talks you through the session. It uses the front camera by default, and you can switch to the back camera to film someone else. I wrote the SwiftUI app and its TypeScript backend on AWS Lambda, and I ran the TestFlight and App Store releases.

This is the first of a few notes on how it is built. I am starting with the decision that shaped most of the others: the camera feed stays on the phone. Video is never recorded, saved or uploaded.

## Why start with that constraint

A rep counter that streams video to a server would be easier in some ways. You could run bigger models and fix counting bugs in one place. But it would mean sending footage of someone's body, often filmed at home, to a server, and every rep would depend on the network.

Keeping the camera local gave me a privacy promise I could state plainly in the App Store listing. It also forced a clear split. The phone owns everything that has to happen in real time: pose tracking, counting, timers and cues. The network is only used for things the workout can carry on without: syncing the log, the trainer chat and the voice coach.

## What runs on the phone

- **Pose tracking.** Google's MediaPipe Pose Landmarker runs on the device with the BlazePose Full model. The model file ships inside the app, so nothing is downloaded at run time and no account is needed. If MediaPipe can't start, or runs below 12 frames a second, that workout switches to Apple's Vision body pose and keeps the count.
- **The rep counter.** It sits on top of the pose stream and counts squats, push-ups and more from the camera. Planks and other holds are timed sets instead: just the time left and a bar.
- **Feedback.** The count is big on screen, and short prerecorded clips in the coach's voice say each number out loud.
- **The log.** Sets are stored on the phone with SwiftData. A finished workout is saved to Apple Health as one workout, and, if the user allows it, heart rate and active calories are read for the set summary.

None of this needs an account. Camera workouts work signed out, and if someone declines camera access the app falls back to manual counting.

## Making the count trustworthy

Most of the work in counting reps from a pose stream is not detecting the movement. It is not counting the things that are not reps. The App Store notes for version 1.0.86 list what I tightened: half reps, walking, standing up and getting into position no longer add reps, and counting keeps going for the whole workout.

Inside the counter, a few rules do most of that work:

- **Each move has a shape.** A squat only counts if the hips go down, the legs move together and the feet stay put. A push-up needs the hands planted below the shoulders. The angle alone can't tell a stride or a knee lift from a squat.
- **Some moves need a posture first.** A row, a press, a curl and a pull-up are all an elbow bending, and so is talking with your hands. So a row only counts once you have been bent over and still for a moment, a pull-up once you are hanging, and a bench press once you are lying back.
- **The first rep waits for a second one.** On squats, curls, presses, rows and deadlifts, a first rep is held for up to 8 seconds and only counts when another follows. A single bend while you talk or tidy up never becomes a set.
- **The range is yours.** The first counted rep records your range, and later reps are judged against a share of it, never easier than a fixed floor.

The rest of the work is around the counter rather than inside it:

- **Get the person in frame first.** A short "3, 2, 1, Go" puts you in position before the clock starts. During the set the screen says what to do (Move back, Come closer, Step into frame), and a setup check before the first set shows the one fix needed if something is off.
- **Always leave a manual path.** If the camera can't see you, minus and plus stay one tap away. With the voice coach on, you can also correct the count by saying something like "That was 10".

Changes to counting were the riskiest changes I made, because a wrong count is the first thing a user notices. So they go out behind feature flags. Camera counting has its own flag, MediaPipe has a switch that sends everyone back to Apple's Vision, and the internal team gets a pose debug overlay that only the server can turn on. Individual behaviours, such as asking for a count confirmation only when the counter is uncertain, can be turned off from the server without waiting for a new build to get through App Review.

I also trained a small rep classifier: gradient-boosted trees on pose features, run on the phone with Core ML. It ships in the app, but it only runs for the internal team, behind a server flag, in shadow mode. It scores every candidate rep and never changes the count, so I can compare it with the live counter on real phones and real sets. The pose model itself is Google's. I haven't trained my own.

## What leaves the phone

The voice coach is the only feature that needs the network during a set, so it is where the boundary had to be drawn most carefully.

The phone opens a WebRTC call to a realtime voice model, and audio goes straight from the phone to the voice service. When the call opens, the backend gives the model your profile, your plan and your recent workouts. During the workout it gets short text notes about the state of the set over the WebRTC data channel and, if Apple Health is on, the set's heart rate and calories. It never gets video frames.

The provider key never reaches the phone. The app asks the backend for a session. A Lambda function checks the feature flags, the user's access and their limits, reads the key from AWS Secrets Manager and mints a short-lived client secret for the call. The secret is only needed to open a call; a call that is already open keeps going. If the connection drops, the app mints a new secret and reconnects quietly, waiting a little longer between tries. While the coach is away, the app's own microphone takes over with Apple's speech recognition, on the device where the phone supports it. Workout commands such as "done" or "take a break" run on the phone, and anything else goes to the trainer as text and the reply is spoken.

Two rules keep the coach from getting in the way:

1. **The coach speaks rarely.** Counts, timers and cues are handled on the phone. The model speaks at the start, at each new exercise, at the end of each set, once during rest if there are at least 20 seconds left, at the end of each exercise and of the workout, and always if you mention pain. By default it gets one line at most in the middle of a set, for form, a stall or leaving the frame.
2. **The model asks; the app acts.** When you say "rest for 90 seconds" or "swap this, my shoulder hurts", the model replies with a tool call rather than changing anything itself. These are some of the workout tools:

```text
log_set          correct_reps     next_exercise    previous_exercise
skip_exercise    swap_exercise    start_rest       extend_rest
skip_rest        pause_workout    resume_workout   change_target
add_set          remove_set       get_workout_status
get_history      get_set_summary  set_coach_style  mute_counts
end_workout
```

The phone runs the tool against its own workout state and shows a small chip with Undo. Larger edits wait for a Confirm, ending the workout waits for a spoken yes, and mentioning pain stops the exercise and offers a swap. The workout state lives on the phone, and the model can only request changes to it.

## The backend, briefly

The backend is TypeScript on AWS Lambda (Node 22, arm64) behind API Gateway and CloudFront, defined with the AWS CDK. It is split into functions by job: one for profile, sessions and sync; one for the trainer and voice tokens; one for sign-in (Better Auth, with Sign in with Apple, Google and email codes); one for operations; and one for subscription webhooks. App data is in six on-demand DynamoDB tables with point-in-time recovery. Sign-in data is in Postgres on Neon.

Two details matter for a phone that is often offline in a gym. Workout uploads and sync honour an `Idempotency-Key` header and keep the response for an hour, so a retried upload doesn't create a duplicate workout. And sync does a last-write-wins merge in batches, so the same log can follow you to another device. A later part covers the data model.

## What I would do the same way again

- **Decide where the boundary is first.** Once "video stays on the phone" was fixed, a lot of other decisions followed from it.
- **Keep the real-time loop on the device.** Counting, timers and cues don't wait for the network, so a slow connection never costs anyone a rep.
- **Let the model ask and the app act.** Tool calls with Undo are easier to reason about, and easier to test, than a model that edits state directly.
- **Ship counting changes behind flags and shadow mode.** If a change started counting badly, I wanted to be able to turn it off from the server, not wait for a new build to clear App Review.
